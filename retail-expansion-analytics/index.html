Scale Dynamics — Target Market (Americas)

Store count and revenue per store, side by side. The FY2025 spike lifted every store on viral demand; the H1 2026 pullback mixes viral normalization with dilution from stores that are still ramping.

FY2024 FY2025 H1 2026 (annualized)

Store Count vs. Revenue per Store

FY2025's revenue-per-store spike reflects a viral tailwind lifting the entire store base. The H1 2026 decline combines that viral fade with new-store dilution — 22 of the 86 stores open at period end had opened within the prior six months and were still on the ramp curve.

Open question. Is the H1 2026 revenue-per-store decline purely viral normalization plus new-store ramp, or is there early cannibalization as store density increases within existing markets? The data available here can't fully separate the two — it needs a same-store cohort view.

Product Category Shift — Global

Category leadership flipped over the period: the largest category in FY2024 is not the largest in H1 2026.

FY2024 FY2025 H1 2026

Category Mix (% of Global Revenue)

Leadership flip. Category B led at 53.2% of revenue in FY2024. By H1 2026, Category A had grown to 57.2% while Category B fell to 30.2% — a full reordering, not just a narrowing gap.

Category C was not separately disclosed as its own line in H1 2026.

Product Line Portfolio — Global, FY2025

The top line still dominates, but the base is broadening: seventeen product lines each cleared ¥100M in FY2025 revenue.

FY2025 Revenue by Product Line (RMB M), with YoY Growth

First crack in the top line. Product Line A drove 38.1% of FY2025 revenue on +365.7% YoY growth, but posted its first year-over-year decline in H1 2026, down 7.5%. Product Line F, meanwhile, grew from a near-zero base to ¥2.06B (+1,602% YoY) — the clearest sign the portfolio is diversifying away from single-line dependence.
